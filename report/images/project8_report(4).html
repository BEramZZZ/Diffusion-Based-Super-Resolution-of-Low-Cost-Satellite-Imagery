Project 8 · Research Report Draft

Diffusion-Based Super-Resolution
of Low-Cost Satellite Imagery

A comparative study of GAN-based and diffusion-based super-resolution, with fine-tuning strategy and hallucination-risk analysis.

Ramzy · USTHB, Algiers
t = 1000 (noise) t = 0 (signal)

01Abstract

Grounded

High-resolution satellite imagery is commercially expensive and infrequently updated; low-cost imagery such as Sentinel-2 (10m/pixel) is free and frequently revisited but too coarse for many analysis tasks. This project investigates generative AI to upscale low-cost imagery into higher-detail products.

Two representative single-image super-resolution model families are compared — a GAN-based model (Real-ESRGAN) and a diffusion-based model (the Stable Diffusion x4 Upscaler) — evaluated zero-shot and against a four-strategy fine-tuning design, using distortion metrics (PSNR, SSIM) alongside a perceptual metric (LPIPS). Some fine-tuning runs are incomplete or come from a later benchmark pass; those run-status differences are documented explicitly in Section 07. Beyond aggregate scores, the project performs a side-by-side qualitative analysis of potential hallucination risk: candidate cases where generated detail may look plausible but conflicts with the reference imagery.

02Objectives

Grounded
  • Build a reproducible benchmarking pipeline comparing a GAN-based and a diffusion-based super-resolution model on real satellite/aerial imagery.
  • Fine-tune both models under four parameter-efficiency strategies and quantify the effect of strategy on output quality and stability.
  • Apply PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS correctly — understanding what each metric does and does not capture.
  • Characterise the tradeoff between perceptual quality and factual fidelity for a trust-sensitive domain.

03Model architectures

Grounded
GAN-based
Real-ESRGAN

RRDBNet generator — 23 Residual-in-Residual Dense Blocks, 64 base channels, no batch normalization. Trained against a U-Net discriminator with spectral normalization. Extends ESRGAN with a second-order degradation model that better approximates real-world (not just bicubic) degradation.

Diffusion-based
Stable Diffusion x4 Upscaler

Latent-diffusion checkpoint specialised for 4× SR. Denoising latents are initialised at the LR image's own pixel resolution; the VAE decoder performs the actual 4× upscaling — a non-standard conditioning scheme documented in full in the source report.

Loss functions and why they produce different behavior

The two models are not just different architectures — they are optimized against fundamentally different objectives. That difference is an important factor in the observed behavior, but the experiments do not isolate it from other factors such as architecture, parameter count, learning rate, conditioning, and run-to-run differences.

Real-ESRGAN fine-tuning
L1 = (1/N) Σ |IHR − ISR|

Direct pixel-wise mean absolute error between the super-resolved output and the real high-resolution image. Note this project's fine-tuning runs use pure L1 — not the original ESRGAN recipe's combined L1 + VGG-perceptual + relativistic-adversarial loss. The zero-shot checkpoint still carries the behavior learned under that fuller objective; fine-tuning nudges it using only L1.

Diffusion fine-tuning
L = E[|| ε − εθ(zt, t) ||²]

Mean squared error between the noise actually added at a randomly sampled timestep t and the noise the UNet predicts, given the noisy latent and the LR conditioning (Section 3.2's 7-channel input). This is not a direct loss on the final image — it's a loss on noise-residual prediction, averaged over the entire timestep schedule.

Why loss values aren't comparable across models

Real-ESRGAN's L1 losses (0.034–0.096) and Diffusion's MSE losses (0.097–0.459) measure different targets and are therefore not numerically comparable across models. Within a model, final training loss is still only an optimization diagnostic: it does not by itself establish validation or test-image quality, especially for a generative diffusion model whose loss supervises noise prediction rather than the final RGB reconstruction. Cross-strategy comparisons are meaningful only when the loss definition and training protocol are held fixed.

Why each model's failure mode looks the way it does

Real-ESRGAN is a deterministic, single-forward-pass regressor. Every training step propagates a direct loss from the reconstructed image, rather than through an iterative denoising trajectory. In the reported runs, the large-scale collapse seen in the earlier diffusion partial run was not reproduced by the ESRGAN full or partial runs; however, ESRGAN head-only also showed a localized wash-out on chaparral, so it is too strong to claim that ESRGAN "never collapses." Its L1 objective favors pixel-wise fidelity and can encourage smoother averages where the input does not uniquely determine high-frequency detail. That provides a plausible explanation for reduced texture, but the experiment does not prove that L1 alone caused each scene-level artifact.

The Diffusion model is an iterative, timestep-dependent denoiser. Its training loss supervises noise prediction at sampled timesteps, while inference applies that prediction repeatedly through a denoising scheduler. This gives the model a mechanism for producing one particular high-frequency realization rather than a single deterministic regression output. It also means that a change in the UNet's prediction can affect the next latent state and therefore the inputs seen at later steps. The earlier Diffusion+partial run (1.51% of parameters) produced a near-uniform output, but a later benchmark pass produced PSNR 26.13 and SSIM 0.663 on the reported comparison scenes. Because these are separate runs, the evidence supports a statement about run sensitivity, not a definitive architecture-level mechanism. The difference could involve optimization details, checkpoint state, objective implementation, inference settings, or other run-specific factors that are not fully logged here.

Both models are evaluated from the same low-resolution input. What happens between "LR in" and "HR out" is where they structurally diverge — one is a single forward pass, the other is an iterative denoising loop whose output can depend on the initial noise and sampling configuration. Stage by stage:

Real-ESRGAN — RRDBNet, single forward pass
01
Input tensor
The LR image is loaded as a 3-channel RGB tensor and its pixel values are scaled to [0,1]. No resizing happens yet — the network ingests the image at its native low resolution.
02
Shallow feature extraction
One 3×3 convolution lifts the 3-channel input to a 64-channel feature map. This map is cached aside — it becomes the long skip connection used in step 04.
03
RRDB trunk — 23 blocks
The features pass through 23 stacked Residual-in-Residual Dense Blocks. Each RRDB nests 3 dense blocks of 5 densely-connected conv layers (growth rate 32); every block's output is scaled by 0.2 before being added back to its own input. No batch normalization anywhere in the trunk — this is also the region "partial" fine-tuning (31%) touches.
04
Global residual connection
The shallow feature map cached in step 02 is added directly onto the trunk's output, so the 23-block trunk only has to learn the residual detail rather than reconstruct the whole feature map from nothing.
05
4× upsampling
Two successive stages of nearest-neighbor upsample + 3×3 conv + LeakyReLU each double the spatial size (2×2=4×). This is the single point in the whole pipeline where the tensor actually grows in height and width.
06
HR refinement convs
Two more 3×3 convolutions, now operating at the full target resolution, sharpen edges and consolidate texture in the upsampled feature map.
07
Output projection
A final 3×3 conv collapses the 64 channels back down to 3 (RGB), producing the finished SR image directly — no intermediate visible states are ever formed.
08
Done — one shot, no randomness
The entire pipeline is one forward pass through ~30 conv layers. The same LR input always produces the exact same HR output, every time.
SD x4 Upscaler — iterative latent denoising
01
Conditioning setup
The LR image is kept at its own native pixel resolution rather than pre-upsampled to the 4× target. A controlled amount of Gaussian noise is mixed into this conditioning image, and the noise level itself is passed to the network as an extra conditioning signal, on top of the timestep.
02
Latent initialization
An initial Gaussian-noise latent with four channels is created at the spatial resolution expected by the upscaler pipeline and scheduler. It is the denoising state zt, not an RGB image; the exact inference timesteps are determined by the configured scheduler rather than requiring the full training endpoint t=1000.
03
Channel concatenation
At every step, the current noisy latent (4 channels) is concatenated with the noise-augmented low-resolution conditioning image (3 channels) to form the 7-channel tensor consumed by the UNet, together with the timestep and noise-level conditioning.
04
Noise prediction
The UNet outputs εθ(zt, t) — its estimate of exactly what noise is present in the current latent, conditioned on the LR image.
05
Sampler step
A sampling schedule (e.g. DDIM/PNDM) uses that noise estimate to step the latent from t to t−Δt, removing a portion of the predicted noise. This repeats for on the order of dozens of steps — the full left-to-right sweep of the timestep strip.
06
Sequential dependency
Each step's output is the next step's input, so the whole trajectory has to stay internally consistent — the direct mechanism behind why heavy fine-tuning destabilizes this model but not Real-ESRGAN (see above).
07
Final decode & 4× upscale
Once denoising is complete, the final latent is passed to the VAE decoder to obtain the RGB image at the pipeline's target output resolution. It is more accurate to describe the VAE as the latent-to-RGB decoder than as a simple standalone 4× interpolation layer.
08
Done — one sample from a distribution
The output depends on the denoising trajectory and, when stochastic initialization or sampling is used, the random seed. With a fixed seed and a deterministic sampler, the same input can be reproduced; different seeds can produce different completions.

05Methodology

Grounded

Metrics — what each one actually captures, and why that matters here

PSNR
Pixel-wise signal fidelity. Sensitive to any deviation, including plausible high-frequency detail.
SSIM
Structural similarity in local windows — closer to perceived structure than raw MSE.
LPIPS
Perceptual distance in a pretrained feature space. Correlates better with human judgement.

PSNR is derived from mean squared pixel error on a logarithmic scale. It measures pixel-wise fidelity to the reference; it does not directly measure perceptual plausibility or factual correctness. Real-ESRGAN's L1 training objective is related to pixel fidelity and often supports stronger PSNR, but L1 and PSNR are not the same optimization objective: L1 minimizes absolute error, whereas PSNR is a monotonic transform of MSE. Diffusion optimizes a noise-prediction objective, so a visually plausible but reference-misaligned texture can receive a lower PSNR even when its perceptual appearance is reasonable.

SSIM compares local luminance, contrast, and structure in sliding windows rather than raw intensity. It's a better proxy for "does this look like the same structure" than PSNR, but it still requires spatial/statistical alignment with the exact ground truth — it does not reward "plausible", only "matching." This is what explains the chaparral reversal in Section 6: Real-ESRGAN's near-flat, under-textured output has almost no local variance, so it fails to structurally correlate with the genuinely high-variance ground-truth texture (SSIM 0.482). Diffusion's output, while not pixel-identical, is itself a high-variance, textured realization — and because SSIM measures structural correlation rather than exact match, generating a plausible textured pattern scores better than generating no texture at all (SSIM 0.648).

LPIPS measures distance in a pretrained deep feature space and is useful as a perceptual complement to PSNR and SSIM. A lower LPIPS can indicate that an output is perceptually closer to the reference even when individual pixels differ. However, LPIPS is not a hallucination detector or a factuality metric. A low perceptual distance cannot establish that an invented road, building, texture, or color corresponds to reality. In this report, hallucination claims therefore require explicit visual/reference verification rather than being inferred from LPIPS alone.

Fine-tuning strategies

Interpretation rule

PSNR/SSIM are reference-based distortion/structure metrics; LPIPS is perceptual similarity. None of the three directly measures factual correctness. Throughout the report, the term hallucination is reserved for detail that is demonstrably unsupported or contradicted by the reference; color shifts, blur, aliasing, and structure loss are reported separately as image artifacts or reconstruction errors.

partial vs. partial

Run-status reconciliation

Two different Diffusion + partial outcomes are present in the project artifacts. The earlier training-log run produced near-uniform outputs, whereas the later benchmark pass reported PSNR 26.13 and SSIM 0.663 on the displayed comparison scenes. These should be treated as separate runs. The report does not contain enough surviving metadata to attribute the difference specifically to an epsilon/v-prediction fix, learning-rate effect, or any other single cause. The earlier collapse is therefore retained as a failed run, not as the expected behavior of the partial strategy.

head_only vs. head_only

Diffusion + head_only shows mainly appearance/tone changes in the displayed examples: a rust/red cast on runway's grass and markings and a faint pink tint through chaparral, while the broad scene layout remains recognizable. This should be described as an empirical visual observation, not as proof that the denoising trajectory is structurally untouched.

ESRGAN + head_only is more limited than a simple "color-filter" description suggests. In the inspected chaparral example it partially washes out texture on one side of the tile. Averaged over the reported 10 scenes, ESRGAN head_only has PSNR 22.82 and SSIM 0.597 versus 24.74 and 0.614 for its zero-shot baseline. This demonstrates that restricting the update to the final projection does not guarantee better reconstruction, even though the earlier feature-extraction layers remain frozen.

lora — ESRGAN only

Diffusion + LoRA produced no output files in this pass (0 of 10 scenes), so no image-based comparison is possible for that benchmark pass. The cause cannot be established from the images alone; the training log should be checked separately. ESRGAN + LoRA reports PSNR 24.12 and SSIM 0.610 and shows a localized chaparral wash-out similar to ESRGAN head-only. The similarity is descriptive; the two strategies modify different parameter sets and the report does not establish a common causal mechanism.

08Hallucination analysis

Grounded — direct image inspection

Side-by-side zero-shot outputs for six scenes, both models on the same LR input. Each case notes where the two outputs diverge and offers an architecture-based interpretation where the evidence supports one.

runway_01 — faithful reconstruction, regular geometry

Real-ESRGAN
Diffusion

Both models reproduce the taxiway layout and the painted "15" runway numbering consistently. Why: geometric, high-contrast, low-entropy content has one clearly correct answer — both a regression-trained GAN and a diffusion model converge on it reliably.

beach_01 — plausible texture invention, not hallucination

Real-ESRGAN
Diffusion

Diffusion renders noticeably more ornate sand-ripple texture and denser, more saturated vegetation coloring than ESRGAN's flatter rendering. Why: this is diffusion's generative flexibility doing exactly what it's designed to do — committing to one specific, plausible high-frequency texture rather than averaging across possibilities. It is not verified against ground truth here, so it should be read as "plausible invented detail," the category the brief specifically asks to distinguish from factually incorrect structure.

forest_00 — largely consistent, minor contrast shift

Real-ESRGAN
Diffusion

Canopy structure agrees between both; diffusion is slightly more vivid and higher-contrast. No structural hallucination observed. Why: dense, fine-grained natural texture with no hard geometric constraint gives both models room to converge on similar-looking, non-identical solutions without either being "wrong."

dense_residential_01 — a small, isolated anomaly

Real-ESRGAN
Diffusion

Road layout and rooftop placement match closely, but the diffusion output shows a small blue/teal blob near the cul-de-sac with no counterpart in the ESRGAN output. Why it is only a candidate anomaly: disagreement with the ESRGAN output is not enough to establish hallucination. The feature must also be checked against the ground-truth/source image. Until that verification is performed, it should be described as a candidate added feature, not a confirmed invented object. This distinction is important because neither PSNR, SSIM, nor LPIPS can certify object-level factual correctness.

Flag — needs verification before citing

river_01 shows a large, unresolved scene discrepancy: the Real-ESRGAN output depicts a desert canyon with a dark river, while the Diffusion output depicts green agricultural fields with structures and a wider canal. Before interpreting this as model hallucination, verify that both outputs were generated from the same LR source and preprocessing path. If the inputs differ, the issue is data/pipeline integrity rather than model behavior. Until that check is completed, this case should remain excluded from hallucination conclusions.

Summary of hallucination-risk findings

  • Real-ESRGAN: no verified object-level hallucination in the inspected fine-tuned examples; recurring aliasing/texture artifacts are documented separately. Head-only also shows a strong appearance shift and a localized chaparral wash-out in the benchmark images.
  • Diffusion zero-shot: the beach and forest cases show plausible texture differences, but those differences should not be labeled hallucinations without reference verification. The dense_residential_01 feature is a candidate anomaly, not a verified hallucination, until checked against the ground truth/source. The river_01 mismatch remains a pairing/integrity issue requiring verification before interpretation.
  • Diffusion + head_only: appearance/tone shifts with broad geometry preserved in the inspected examples. The effect should not be described as a color-only mapping or as guaranteed structural preservation because the trainable layers alter the predicted noise residual used throughout denoising.
  • Diffusion + partial: the earlier run produced output collapse, while the later benchmark pass produced meaningful reconstructions. The report therefore records run sensitivity rather than a universal "structure erasure" conclusion.

09Limitations & future work

Grounded
  • The draft should report the exact evaluation protocol for reproducibility: test-pair count, train/validation/test split, crop/resize and degradation settings, inference steps, scheduler, guidance/noise settings, random seed policy, and hardware. These details are not fully specified in the present report, so the quantitative results should be interpreted as benchmark results for the current run configuration rather than universally reproducible model properties.
  • ControlNet was considered as a candidate addition per the brief's "potentially" framing, but not integrated, due to time constraints on top of the core benchmarking and fine-tuning comparison.
  • Diffusion + full fine-tuning was later run in a follow-up benchmark pass (see the strategy comparison in Section 07); no trainable-param/loss log survives from that run, so it is not included in the training-log table — only its generated outputs and computed image metrics are available. Diffusion + LoRA produced no output files in that same pass; the cause is unresolved from the available artifacts.
  • Dataset revision pending Fine-tuning used RESISC45/NAIP rather than genuine Sentinel-2-to-high-resolution paired imagery; this dataset/protocol decision is intentionally left unchanged for now and will be revised separately.
