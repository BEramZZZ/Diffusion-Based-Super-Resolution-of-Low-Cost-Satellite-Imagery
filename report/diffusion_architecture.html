Where invention enters — the prompt. This run's fixed prompt, DEFAULT_PROMPT = "a high resolution aerial satellite photograph, sharp detail, realistic terrain" (see the settings table above), describes a look, not information read off the LR pixels. Cross-attention lets that description steer every denoising step, so whatever "sharp" and "realistic" mean inside the model's learned prior is what fills the missing high-frequency content, independent of whether it matches the actual terrain.

Walking the forward pass, block by block

The pipeline conditions on two separate signals at once: a text prompt, encoded by a fixed, pretrained OpenCLIP-ViT/H text encoder and fed to the UNet's cross-attention layers, as in text-to-image Stable Diffusion, and the low-resolution (LR) image, which is noise-augmented and concatenated onto the latent instead of going through cross-attention. It also runs the diffusion process at the LR image's own pixel resolution instead of the heavily-downsampled latent space ordinary Stable Diffusion uses. That resolution choice is the key structural difference from a standard SD checkpoint: there's no separate VAE encoder step for the conditioning image, and the spatial ×4 jump happens exactly once, at the very end, inside a VAE decoder retrained specifically for this task.

Conditioning path
Noise-augment the LR image

A controlled amount of Gaussian noise is mixed into the LR input before it's used as conditioning, and the model is told exactly how much via a separate noise-level embedding. This makes the upscaler more tolerant of LR images that weren't produced by the exact same degradation pipeline used during training — a trick borrowed from cascaded / SR3-style super-resolution diffusion models.

Generative path
Pure-noise latent init

z_T starts as standard Gaussian noise, 4 channels, sized to the LR image's own resolution (not 8× downsampled). Over the sampling loop this noise is progressively reshaped into the missing high-frequency detail the model has learned to hallucinate.

Text conditioning path
Prompt → OpenCLIP → cross-attention

The prompt is encoded once, up front, into 1024-dim embeddings; those don't change across the 30 steps. They never touch the concat — they reach the UNet only as the key/value pairs its cross-attention layers read in levels L2–L4 and the mid block (L1 has no attention; see the UNet zoom).

At every timestep, the noisy latent and the noise-augmented conditioning image are stacked channel-wise — 4ch + 3ch → 7ch — and fed into the UNet together with two scalar conditioning signals: the diffusion timestep t and the LR noise-augmentation level (inside the UNet these are summed into a single embedding; see the zoom below). The fixed text embeddings from OpenCLIP ride along unchanged at every one of the 30 steps, read by cross-attention rather than joining the concat; the sampler also runs the UNet a second time per step with an empty prompt for classifier-free guidance (see the sampler zoom). The UNet's output is a v-prediction, vθ(z_t, t): a blend of noise and clean signal rather than the noise itself, because the released scheduler configuration for this checkpoint is set to v-prediction. A DDIM step then converts that output into estimates of x_0 and ε and uses them to compute a slightly-less-noisy z_(t−Δt). With η=0, DDIM is a deterministic, ODE-style solver, which is what lets the loop run in 30 steps rather than the full 1000 timesteps the model was trained across.

That z feeds straight back into the next concat, so the loop is self-contained: the LR conditioning stays fixed, only the latent evolves. Once t reaches 0, the loop exits and the final latent passes through the specially-trained VAE decoder, which performs the actual ×4 spatial upscale to produce the HR output. Everything before that point — noise-augment, concat, UNet, sampler — runs at a single, fixed spatial resolution; the upscaling factor lives entirely in that final decode step.

Where invention enters — the prior. The UNet was trained on a broad web-image dataset (a LAION subset, per the model card), not on satellite or aerial data specifically, and the OpenCLIP text encoder it listens to was trained on web image–text pairs. Applied zero-shot to RESISC45 / NAIP, the model fills in missing detail from that learned prior — a source of invented content that exists even with no prompt and no guidance.

Down / mid / up, and what the skip connections are actually doing

The UNet has four resolution levels. On the way down it runs two ResBlocks per level and halves the spatial size after each of the first three, while channel width grows 256 → 512 → 512 → 1024; the mid block then works on the same 1/8-size grid as the deepest level. That compression gives the network a large receptive field, which matters for predicting v consistently across big regions instead of in disconnected patches. Attention is not uniform across levels, going by the released config: level 1 has none, levels 2 and 3 use attention layers that attend only to the text embeddings (the config sets only_cross_attention for them, so there is no image self-attention there), and level 4 combines self-attention over the image with cross-attention to the text. The mid block has attention of its own.

The up path mirrors it: four levels again, but three ResBlocks per level, and each ResBlock concatenates in a skip tensor of matching resolution before it runs. The horizontal arrows are channel-wise concatenation, not addition, and each one stands for three skip tensors: that level's two down-path ResBlock outputs plus the previous level's downsample output (the input-conv output, for level 1). That is how the up path recovers fine spatial detail the bottleneck would otherwise discard, at the cost of a wider input to each ResBlock. Levels 4, 3 and 2 upsample on the way up; level 1 does not.

The two scalar conditioning signals are not injected as separate embeddings. The noise level enters as a class-label embedding (the config declares 1000 classes) that is added to the timestep embedding, and the summed embedding is projected and added to the feature maps inside every ResBlock, down, mid and up. That is an additive bias, not AdaGN-style scale-and-shift modulation. The output layer maps back to 4 channels — the same shape as the input latent — producing vθ, which the sampler step combines with z_t.

The cross-attention layers attend to the text-encoder embeddings (cross-attention dimension 1024), which is how the prompt conditions the denoiser, while the LR image enters only through the concatenation at the input. That text embedding is computed once, up front, from the prompt, and stays fixed across all 30 steps — it's the dashed bus in the diagram above, tapping into every cross-attention layer at L2, L3, L4 and the mid block (L1 gets no text conditioning at all). During the unconditional guidance pass the same bus simply carries an empty-prompt embedding instead (see the sampler zoom).

Where invention enters — guidance. vg = vu + w·(vc−vu) extrapolates past the unconditional prediction along the direction the prompt moves it, for every w > 1, so a higher guidance scale gives the prompt more influence over the result. The LR image is fed to both passes, so guidance amplifies only the prompt's contribution. Whether that shows up as more invented detail is something to test rather than assume: re-run the same images at a few values of w (w=9.0 in this run, per the settings table above) and compare.

One step, and why fewer than 1000 of them is enough

Training defines the diffusion process across 1000 discrete timesteps, but sampling doesn't have to visit all of them. The schedule strip above shows the idea: the DDIM scheduler picks a much shorter subsequence — 30 steps in this project — spread across the full t:1000→0 range, and the sampler only ever runs the UNet at those chosen points. This works because DDIM-style samplers treat denoising as solving a deterministic ODE rather than the strictly step-by-step Markov chain used during training; skipping most of the intermediate points introduces only a small discretization error, which is the trade-off being made for speed.

Each of those 30 steps also runs the UNet twice, not once. A conditional pass (vc) reads the prompt's embeddings through cross-attention, and an unconditional pass (vu) reads an empty prompt instead, with everything else — z_t, t, the LR conditioning — held identical between the two. Classifier-free guidance then combines the pair into a single guided prediction, vg = vu + w·(vc−vu), where w is the guidance scale chosen for this run: w=1 would just take the conditional pass on its own, and larger w pushes the result further in the direction the prompt moves it, at the usual cost of over-saturated or over-sharpened output if pushed too far. It's this guided vg, not either pass alone, that the rest of the step is built from.

The network predicts v, not noise, so the sampler first converts the guided vg, writing ᾱ_t for the cumulative product of the noise schedule (α_t in the DDIM paper): x_0 ≈ √ᾱ_t·z_t − √(1−ᾱ_t)·vg, and ε̂ = √(1−ᾱ_t)·z_t + √ᾱ_t·vg. The next latent is then assembled from the two: z_(t−Δt) = √ᾱ_(t−Δt)·x_0 + √(1−ᾱ_(t−Δt))·ε̂, where the second term is the "direction" term. With η=0 no fresh random noise is injected between steps, which is what makes DDIM deterministic. Identical output additionally requires the same seed for the starting latent z_T and for the noise added to the LR conditioning image, since that augmentation noise is also drawn at inference time.

Real-ESRGAN vs. this pipeline: point estimate vs. sampling from p(HR|LR)

"Deterministic vs. iterative" isn't the right way to draw this comparison — DDIM at η=0 is itself deterministic given a fixed seed, as the caveat box above notes, so both pipelines can be made to return the same output on the same input twice. The real distinction is what each one is estimating. Real-ESRGAN is trained as a direct regression: one forward pass maps a given LR image to one HR output, full stop — there's no notion of "a different but equally valid" output for the same input. This pipeline instead samples from a learned distribution over plausible reconstructions, p(HR|LR): the same LR image, prompt and guidance scale, but a different starting latent zT or a different LR-noise-augmentation seed, can produce a different HR image that's still a plausible sample from that distribution.

That's also why "hallucination" means something different for the two. Real-ESRGAN can also invent plausible texture (it is trained with an adversarial loss alongside L1 and perceptual losses), but for a given input it invents the same thing every time, so its output is one fixed answer that is either close to the true HR image or not. Any one sample from p(HR|LR), by contrast, can be a perfectly typical draw from the distribution the model learned and still not match the specific ground truth — sampling variance, not just model error, is baked into what this pipeline is doing.

Where invention enters — the noise level. The checkpoint takes the augmentation level as an input, so it is a knob at inference time (20 in this run, per the settings table above). Training is one regression step, not a loop

Every other diagram on this page describes what happens at inference time, where the sampler iterates 30 times to turn pure noise into a sharp image. Training looks almost nothing like that. For each (HR, LR) pair in a batch, the model draws one random timestep t and one random noise vector ε, uses the closed-form forward-diffusion equation to jump directly to the corresponding noisy latent z_t — no denoising loop involved — and asks the UNet to predict the matching v target in a single forward pass. The same UNet, the same 7-channel concat, and the same timestep/noise-level embeddings from every other diagram here are reused unchanged; only how t is chosen differs (random instead of following a fixed schedule). Unverified The conditioning noise level is expected to be randomised per pair as well, but that has not been checked against the training code.

The loss is a v-prediction objective (Salimans & Ho, 2022), not the plain noise-prediction loss of the original DDPM. With z_t = √ᾱ·z_0 + √(1−ᾱ)·ε, the UNet is trained to output v = √ᾱ·ε − √(1−ᾱ)·z_0, and the loss is the mean squared error between that target and vθ. The dashed paths in the diagram carry z_0, t and the sampled ε into the target. It is still self-supervised: nothing beyond the paired HR/LR images is needed. This page takes the objective from the released scheduler configuration for this checkpoint, which specifies v-prediction.

Gradients from that loss flow back into the UNet's weights. Unverified The VAE is conventionally pretrained separately and kept frozen at this stage, which would make the UNet the only piece updated here. That is carried over from how latent-diffusion models are usually trained and has not been checked against this checkpoint's training code.
